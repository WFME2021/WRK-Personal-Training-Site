Is It Worth Hiring a Personal Trainer? An Honest Answer After 20 Years

Wondering if a personal trainer is worth the money? A Christchurch trainer with 20 years' experience gives the honest answer, including who it's not for.

Is It Worth Hiring a Personal Trainer? An Honest Answer After 20 Years

If you're asking whether a personal trainer is worth it, you're already doing the maths. Good. That's the right question to ask before you spend money on anything.

So here's the honest version, from someone who has coached for 20 years and will tell you straight.

A good trainer is the difference between a plan that gets you the result you actually want, in a reasonable timeframe, and falling on and off the wagon for years because your programme never fit your life in the first place.

That second one is expensive too. It just doesn't send you an invoice.

What you're really paying for

Most people picture a personal trainer as someone who gets you buff. We can do that. But that's not the job.

The job is to build training around your current capabilities and constraints, and to keep you moving toward your goal in a way you can actually sustain. Tailored. Adaptable. Built for the week your life goes sideways, not just the week it goes to plan.

The bulk of my in-person clients run companies or run households. Often both. They don't need me to be a cheerleader. They need a plan that bends when their week does.

A real example. Scott.

Scott came to me wanting to prep for the upcoming cricket season. He'd picked up a few niggles over the past couple of years. The joys of getting older with a desk job that keeps you sitting all day.

He knew he wanted to move better. He just didn't know where to start.

And he almost didn't come, because he thought personal training was all about getting buff, and he didn't want to pay for that.

Here's what I showed him. A good trainer does three things.

Assess. Movement screens and postural assessments, so we know exactly what your body is doing right now.

Address. We work through the day-to-day obstacles and the objections. The niggles, the schedule, the things getting in the way.

Customise. I prescribe a plan built around those considerations and guide you through the sticking points until you reach the goal.

Once Scott understood that coaching gets tailored to whatever he needs, he was on board. He's a regular weekly client now.

What a gym membership, an app, or YouTube can't give you

A membership gives you a room full of equipment. An app gives you a plan that has never met you. Neither of them knows how you slept, what's flared up this week, or that you've got a condition that makes half the "standard" exercises a bad idea.

That's the gap.

With a trainer you get a session that adjusts to how you're actually feeling on the day. You get someone who pivots the plan when life happens, so you keep progressing instead of stalling. And for a lot of my busy clients, the real value is simple. You come in, you train, you're in good hands, and you don't have to think about it. The workout is taken care of.

It's also not just strength work. A lot of my clients carry injuries or medical considerations. Exercise helps them. The wrong exercise doesn't, and can put them at risk. Knowing the difference is the whole point.

Where people get the "worth it" maths wrong

In-person training is a financial commitment. No dodging that.

I've worked with the corporate market for 20 years, and the people who see it clearest understand one thing. Being a high performer means keeping yourself in good health. The cost of training is outweighed by the cost of not being able to perform.

The other thing worth knowing is that a big part of my job is being a solution finder. If the one-on-one price point doesn't fit right now, that's not the end of the conversation. There are workarounds that still get you a tailored approach:

  • Paired sessions. Train with someone else and share the cost, without losing the personalised programming.
  • Small group. Still tailored solutions, at a shared price.
  • Online coaching. A personalised training approach and regular touchpoints, minus the weekly in-person commitment.

Same standard. Different door in.

The money, straight

Here's what it costs, so you can weigh it properly.

  • In-person, one-on-one: $55 per 30-minute session.
  • Online: $99 for your programme design, then $14.95 per week on a 16-week commitment.

Thirty minutes of specific, technical work is enough. Your warm-up, your activation routine, and the tailored finishers are things you can run yourself. You don't need to pay someone to stand around and talk about your feelings while you do cardio.

Now weigh that against what not getting results is already costing you. The gym subs you don't use. The apps and programmes that went nowhere. The years of stop-start. Most people have already spent more than a term of coaching on things that didn't move the needle.

So, is it worth it for you?

If you're 35-plus, running a house or a business, time-poor, and you've either tried and stalled on your own or been burned by a trainer who over-promised, then yes. You're not lazy. You're busy, and you've got every reason to be unconvinced.

The honest answer to "is it worth it" is that it depends on getting the right fit for where you are right now. So start there.

Frequently Asked Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

How often do I need to train with a PT to see results?

For most people, two to three quality sessions a week moves the needle. But it's not all with me. A single focused session, plus the warm-up and finishers you run yourself, can be enough to make real progress. It comes down to your goal and your starting point, which is exactly what we work out up front.

Do I need to already be fit before I start?

No. That's backwards. You get fit by starting, not before you start. Every plan begins with an assessment of where you are right now, and we build from there. Wherever you're at, that's the starting line.

Can you train around an injury or a medical condition?

Yes. This is a core part of the job, not something to hide. We assess it, build the programme around it, and where it makes sense we work alongside your physio, GP or specialist. The wrong exercise can set you back. The right one, prescribed properly, is often part of getting you better.

How long until I actually see results?

It depends on your goal, and I won't hand you a number that isn't real. What I'll tell you is this. Most people feel the difference first. More energy, better movement, less stiffness, usually within the first few weeks. The visible and measurable changes take longer and come down to consistency. Show up, do the work, and the results follow.

In-person or online, which is right for me?

In-person suits you if you want hands-on coaching, technical work, and the accountability of a booked session you show up to. Online suits you if you want a personalised plan and regular touchpoints but need to train on your own schedule. Not sure which fits? Do the fitness assessment and it'll point you to the right one.

— Hayden
